What makes a report easy to fix

You don't need all of these — but the more you include, the faster it gets sorted.

  • The item number or card text. Every card back shows its source — e.g. “MedQA train split, item #…” Copy that line if you can.
  • What you expected vs. what the card says. “Answer says B, but the stem points to C because…” beats “this card is wrong.”
  • Your setup for tech issues. Anki version, phone or desktop, and what happened when you imported.
  • A screenshot, if it helps. The form can't take attachments — mention in your message that you have one, and Andy can ask for it in his reply.

What you're supporting

The deck turns the MedQA train split into study cards — clinical vignettes in the “Are you smarter than a healthbot?” spirit: read the stem, commit to an answer, then check yourself on the back.

Card structureFront → Back

Front — a clinical vignette with four answer options.

Back — the correct answer, the source line with the MedQA item number, and the Pivot–Trap grading rubric: Again if you missed the pivot or fell for the trap, Good when you named the pivot, Easy when you named it and rejected the trap cold.

Decks: MedQA::Step 1 (5,629 cards) and MedQA::Step 2-3 (4,547 cards). Train split only — the test split is held out, so nothing in the deck spoils it.

Before you write — quick answers

Three things that come up most often.

A card's answer looks wrong. What should I do?

Send it in with the form — include the item number from the card back and, in one or two lines, why you think it's off. Answer-key and stem problems are exactly what this page is for, and they're logged and checked against the source item.

The deck won't import, or cards look broken.

Say which file you imported (Step 1 or Step 2–3), your Anki version, and the exact error message if there is one. Long words or formatting looking off on a phone is also worth reporting — include your device.

How does the Pivot–Trap scheduling work?

Grade honestly against the rubric on each card back. A lapse where you missed the pivot or fell for the trap brings the card back sooner; naming the pivot and rejecting the trap earns the full interval. If your intervals look off, report your deck name — the scheduling keys off it.

Can I suggest a fix or a new feature?

Yes — pick “Suggestion” in the form. Corrections, clearer wording, and deck ideas all go to the same inbox and get read.
